Behind every withdrawal at another bank's ATM, every card payment at a merchant, lies an invisible but essential infrastructure: the **card switch**. For Ivorian banks and payment institutions, mastering it is a major competitive advantage.

## What is a card switch?

A card switch is a system that **routes and authorizes payment transactions in real time** between different players: issuing banks, acquiring banks, card networks, mobile money operators and merchants.

Concretely, when a customer uses their card on a terminal, the switch:

1. Receives the authorization request,
2. Routes it to the issuing bank,
3. Checks fund availability,
4. Returns the response (approved or declined) within seconds.

All of this in a **secure and traceable** way, with a very high availability requirement.

## The role of GIM-WAEMU

The **GIM-WAEMU** (Interbank Monetics Group of WAEMU) is the body ensuring **monetics interoperability** between the banks of the union's eight countries, including Côte d'Ivoire. Thanks to it, one bank's card can be used on another bank's ATM or terminal, at a regional scale.

For an Ivorian bank, being properly interconnected to the GIM means:

- Offering customers a broader acceptance network,
- Generating revenue on interbank transactions,
- Contributing to regional financial inclusion.

## Domestic switch and mobile money interoperability

Beyond cards, the current challenge is **interoperability between mobile money and bank accounts**. A modern switch must be able to interconnect:

- Card networks (GIM-WAEMU, international schemes),
- Mobile money operators,
- Interbank systems,
- Payment aggregators and FinTechs.

It is this convergence that creates truly seamless payment journeys for the end customer.

## The technical requirements of a reliable switch

A card switch only has value if it is flawless on three points:

### Availability
Transactions never stop. The architecture must aim for **high availability** (redundancy, automatic failover) to avoid any service interruption.

### Security
End-to-end encryption, key management, compliance with payment security standards: trust rests on security.

### Performance
Each transaction must be authorized within a few hundred milliseconds, even during peak periods.

## What this means for Ivorian players

Mastering your monetics — rather than depending entirely on external providers — is a matter of **sovereignty and margin**. Banks and payment institutions that invest in a modern, well-interconnected switch infrastructure take control of their transactions, their costs and their customer experience.
