Cyberattacks no longer target only large multinationals. In Côte d'Ivoire, SMEs, banks, public bodies and microfinance institutions are increasingly exposed targets — payment fraud, ransomware, data theft, social engineering. The good news: most incidents can be avoided with structured measures.

## Why the threat is rising

The accelerated digitalization of Ivorian businesses mechanically widens the attack surface: mobile money, online payments, remote work, cloud. In parallel, regulation is tightening with personal data protection framed by the **ARTCI**. Doing nothing exposes you to a double risk: financial and regulatory.

## The 7 essential measures

### 1. Map your risks
You only protect well what you know. A **security audit** identifies your critical assets, your vulnerabilities and prioritizes them. It is the starting point of any strategy.

### 2. Secure access
Most intrusions exploit weak or stolen passwords. Put in place:

- **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)**,
- A strong password policy,
- The principle of least privilege (everyone only accesses what they need).

### 3. Encrypt sensitive data
Financial and personal data must be encrypted, at rest and in transit. In case of theft, encrypted data remains unusable.

### 4. Back up and test recovery
A regular, isolated and **tested** backup is the best protection against ransomware. A backup that has never been tested is not a backup.

### 5. Secure payments
For financial players, end-to-end security of transactions and monetics flows is vital: anomaly monitoring, fraud detection, network segmentation.

### 6. Train your teams
People remain the first attack vector. Regular awareness sessions (phishing, best practices) drastically reduce risk.

### 7. Achieve ARTCI compliance
Beyond technical protection, compliance with Ivorian personal data regulation is an obligation. It also structures your security governance.

## Detect and respond, not just prevent

No protection is infallible. Mature organizations complement prevention with a **detection and incident response** capability: continuous monitoring, a response plan, and regular drills. The question is not "if" but "when" — and reaction speed makes all the difference.

## In summary

Cybersecurity is not a product you buy once, but a continuous discipline. For Ivorian businesses, it has become a condition of trust — toward customers, partners and the regulator.
