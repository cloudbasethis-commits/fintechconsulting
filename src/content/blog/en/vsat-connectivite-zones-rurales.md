Opening a bank branch in a secondary town, equipping a mining or agricultural site, connecting a distant warehouse: in Côte d'Ivoire, connectivity remains a challenge as soon as you leave the major urban centers. Yet reliable solutions exist.

## The problem: uneven coverage

Fiber optic and 4G cover Abidjan and the main cities well, but leave entire areas poorly served. However, a branch or industrial site needs a **reliable and permanent** connection to operate: transactions, monitoring, communications, security.

## Connectivity solutions

### VSAT (satellite)
**VSAT** (Very Small Aperture Terminal) provides broadband connectivity **via satellite**, independent of terrestrial infrastructure. It is the ideal solution for areas not covered by fiber or 4G: mining sites, agricultural sites, rural branches. Major advantage: it works **everywhere**, including the most remote areas.

### Fiber optic
Where available, fiber offers the best throughput and latency. It is the reference solution for urban sites and headquarters.

### Radio links (microwave)
Radio links interconnect two relatively close sites without digging trenches, with good throughput. Useful to link buildings or bypass an obstacle.

## Interconnecting several sites

A multi-site business needs more than a simple Internet connection: it must **interconnect its sites securely**. Private networks (VPN, MPLS) link branches, headquarters and industrial sites into a unified network, as if they were on the same local network.

## Staying in control: monitoring

A link that goes down without anyone knowing means a halted operation. Proactive **link monitoring** allows you to:

- Detect incidents before they impact operations,
- Measure the quality of service actually delivered,
- Anticipate capacity needs.

## Choosing the right combination

In practice, the best architecture often combines several technologies: fiber where it exists, VSAT for isolated sites, radio links as a complement, all monitored and securely interconnected.
